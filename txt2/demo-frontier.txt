 Theorem 6: the clipped frontier

 For the second-order surrogate, the optimal coupling is the clipped inverse
 of t(&gamma;) = &minus;V&#8320;&prime;/G&prime;. If t sweeps (0,&infin;) the
 optimizer sweeps the whole bridge (Family A). If t reaches a finite limit
 t&#8320; at the HRP end, then for s = &tau;&sup2; &ge; t&#8320; the surrogate
 parks at HRP (Family B, with t&#8320; = 27/800).

 Left: t(&gamma;) for both families (log scale), computed
 from the recursion. Right: the resulting surrogate optimizer
 &gamma;&#8322;*(s); Family A sweeps the bridge, Family B clips to 0 at
 s = 27/800.

 What to look for: Family A's t diverges at the left end
 and vanishes at the right (no clipping); Family B's t hits a ceiling at 27/800
 &asymp; 0.034, and its optimizer path drops to zero there. A Family B optimizer
 that stayed positive past s = 27/800 would refute the clipping.
