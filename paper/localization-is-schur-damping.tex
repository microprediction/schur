\begin{abstract}
Ensemble Kalman filters damp the cross-covariances of a small-ensemble
forecast covariance by an elementwise product with a distance taper. We
observe that a taper which is constant on blocks is the Schur-complementary
damping of \cite{cotton2024schur}: the localized covariance is positive
semidefinite, its conditional covariance of the unobserved block is the
damped Schur complement $A-\gamma BD^{-1}B^\top$, and the localized gain is
$\sqrt{\gamma}$ times the optimal gain. Under sampling error in the
cross-covariance the expected analysis error is an exact quadratic in
$\sqrt\gamma$, so the optimal coupling is strictly interior for every noise
level and has the closed form of a signal-to-noise shrinkage of the gain.
\end{abstract}

\section{Setting}

Partition a state into an unobserved block and an observed block, with
forecast error covariance
$\Sigma=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}A&B\\B^\top&D\end{smallmatrix}\right)\succ0$.
Observations of the second block carry noise of covariance $R$, so the
innovation has covariance $S=D+R$. The optimal gain for the unobserved block
is $K_\star=BS^{-1}$ and its analysis error covariance is $A-BS^{-1}B^\top$.
With $R=0$ this is the Schur complement of $A$ in $\Sigma$.

An ensemble Kalman filter \cite{evensen1994} replaces $\Sigma$ by a sample
covariance from a small ensemble. Because the sample cross-covariances are
noisy, the filter localizes: it multiplies the sample covariance elementwise
by a taper $C$ that decays with distance
\cite{houtekamer2001,hamill2001,gaspari1999}. The product $\Sigma\circ C$
is positive semidefinite whenever $C$ is, by the Schur product theorem. The
name is Schur's, but the operation is the elementwise product, and the
strength of localization is chosen empirically.

\section{A block-constant taper is Schur damping}

Fix $\gamma\in[0,1]$ and let $C_\gamma$ equal $1$ on the two diagonal blocks
and $\sqrt\gamma$ on the two off-diagonal blocks.

\begin{proposition}[Block taper]
\label{prop:taper}
$C_\gamma$ is positive semidefinite, so $\Sigma\circ C_\gamma\succeq0$. The
conditional covariance of the unobserved block in $\Sigma\circ C_\gamma$ is
the damped Schur complement
\begin{equation}
  A-\gamma\,BD^{-1}B^\top
  \label{eq:damped}
\end{equation}
of \cite{cotton2024schur}, and the localized gain is $\sqrt\gamma\,K_\star$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$C_\gamma=\sqrt\gamma\,\ones\ones^\top+(1-\sqrt\gamma)\,
(\ones_A\ones_A^\top+\ones_D\ones_D^\top)$ is a nonnegative combination of
rank-one positive semidefinite matrices. The tapered matrix has blocks $A$,
$\sqrt\gamma B$ and $D$, so its Schur complement is
$A-\sqrt\gamma B\,D^{-1}\sqrt\gamma B^\top$, and its gain is
$\sqrt\gamma\,BS^{-1}$.
\end{proof}

So a block-constant localization is the coupling dial of Schur-complementary
allocation. No localization is $\gamma=1$ and complete localization is
$\gamma=0$, the ends of the bridge in \cite{cotton2024schur,cotton2026bridge}.
Distance tapers vary within blocks and are not of this form; the block case
is the one in which the complement description is exact.

\section{The optimal coupling under sampling error}

Let the cross-covariance be estimated with error, $\widehat B=B+\tau E$,
where $E$ has mean zero, a law unchanged by $E\mapsto-E$, and is
independent of the forecast and observation errors. Take $S$ as known. The
localized filter applies the gain $s\,\widehat BS^{-1}$ with $s=\sqrt\gamma$.

\begin{proposition}[Exact expected analysis error]
\label{prop:optimal}
The expected analysis error covariance of the unobserved block is
\begin{equation}
  F(s)=A-(2s-s^2)\,Q+s^2\tau^2\,G,
  \qquad Q=BS^{-1}B^\top,\quad G=\E\big[ES^{-1}E^\top\big].
  \label{eq:F}
\end{equation}
Its trace is minimized at
\begin{equation}
  s_\star=\frac{\tr Q}{\tr Q+\tau^2\tr G},
  \qquad
  \gamma_\star=s_\star^2 .
  \label{eq:sstar}
\end{equation}
If $\tr G>0$ then $\gamma_\star<1$ for every $\tau>0$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
With gain $K$ the analysis error covariance is
$A-KB^\top-BK^\top+KSK^\top$. Put $K=s(B+\tau E)S^{-1}$ and take
expectations. The terms linear in $E$ vanish by sign symmetry, the cross
terms vanish by independence, and $\E[KSK^\top]=s^2(Q+\tau^2G)$. The trace
of \eqref{eq:F} is a convex quadratic in $s$ with minimizer
\eqref{eq:sstar}.
\end{proof}

The optimal gain is the sample gain shrunk by $s_\star$, the ratio of the
variance the true gain removes to that quantity plus the variance the
sampling error adds. This is a sampling-error correction in the sense of
\cite{anderson2012}, applied uniformly to a block rather than entry by
entry. The Schur coupling is its square. With independent standard normal
entries in $E$, $G=\tr(S^{-1})\,I$.

\begin{remark}[Contrast with the portfolio bridge]
On the portfolio bridge the full-coupling end can be the out-of-sample
optimum or not, according to the sign of one derivative there
\cite{cotton2026bridge,cotton2026nco}. Here the optimum is interior for
every noise level. The reason is that the analysis mean is linear in the
gain and there is no budget constraint, so the objective is an exact
quadratic in $s$, and the companion vector of the allocation problem has no
counterpart.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Scope]
Taking $S$ as known and $E$ independent of the errors are simplifications.
In an ensemble filter the sampling errors of $\widehat B$ and $\widehat D$
come from the same ensemble and are correlated with each other and with the
forecast errors. \Cref{prop:taper} is exact regardless. \Cref{prop:optimal}
is the leading-order model of the trade-off, and the quantities in
\eqref{eq:sstar} are estimable from the ensemble. We have not found the
complement formulation in the data assimilation literature; the nearest
relatives are the sampling-error corrections of \cite{anderson2007,anderson2012}.
\end{remark}
