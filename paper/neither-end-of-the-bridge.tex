\begin{abstract}
Schur-complementary allocation interpolates hierarchical risk parity
($\gamma = 0$) and minimum variance ($\gamma \to 1$) through a coupling
$\gamma$ that sets how much of the estimated cross-block covariance the
recursion uses. We ask where on the bridge to sit when the covariance is
estimated with error, and answer in three layers. A structural theorem
shows that top-level cross-block noise is invisible at the hierarchical end
and prices coupling at order $\gamma\tau^2$, so small noise moves the
optimum uniformly away from hierarchical risk parity whenever coupling has
population value. A local theorem at an exact minimum-variance endpoint
shows that positive marginal noise sensitivity pushes the optimum into the
interior. An exchangeable four-asset family is then solved exactly: its
unique optimizer is interior at every admissible noise level and decreases
monotonically from full coupling to the middle of the bridge. Exact
examples mark the boundaries of what holds in general, and every exact
claim is verified in rational arithmetic.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} portfolio construction; hierarchical risk parity;
Schur complement; estimation error; shrinkage; bias--variance trade-off.\\

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Hierarchical risk parity \cite{lopezdeprado2016} allocates by recursive
bisection without inverting a covariance matrix. Minimum variance inverts it
in full. The two are ends of a single construction: augment each block with
a $\gamma$-scaled Schur complement of its sibling and the recursion is
exactly HRP at $\gamma = 0$, while recovering minimum variance as
$\gamma \to 1$. This unification first appeared in a blog
\cite{cotton2022blog}, then in a textbook \cite[\S12.3.4]{palomar2025}, with
a fuller development in \cite{cotton2024schur}. It quickly found its way
into practice: \texttt{skfolio}, a popular Python portfolio library, ships
it as \texttt{SchurComplementary}. On the hierarchical graph structures of
\cite{mograby2025} the $\gamma = 1$ recovery is exact. The endpoints have
since been compared analytically: \cite{antonov2025} derive the weight noise
of HRP and of Markowitz under covariance estimation error and prove HRP is
the more robust.

The question behind the bridge is a form of the classical estimation-risk
problem. \cite{jobson1980} quantified the sampling error of efficient
portfolios; \cite{michaud1989} named the enigma; \cite{jagannathanma2003}
showed that imposing the wrong constraints acts as shrinkage and helps;
\cite{demiguel2009} documented how hard the $1/N$ portfolio is to beat; and
\cite{ledoitwolf2004} made shrinkage of the input systematic. The Schur
coupling gives that trade-off a single parameter, and the present paper
gives the parameter provable structure.

This paper asks where on the bridge one should sit when the covariance is
estimated with error, and answers in three layers. (i)~\emph{The HRP end,
in general.} Top-level cross-block noise is invisible at $\gamma = 0$, and
its expected effect factors exactly as $F - V_0 = \gamma \tau^2 H$ with $H$
bounded (Theorem~\ref{thm:noisecost}). Whenever coupling has first-order
population value, the minimizer is uniformly separated from HRP for all
sufficiently small noise (Theorem~\ref{thm:nohrp}). The order
$\gamma\tau^2$ cannot be improved to $\gamma^2\tau^2$: an exact example has
boundary derivative $-\tfrac{1}{700} + \tfrac{8}{189}\tau^2$, so HRP becomes
a one-sided local minimum at the finite noise level
$\tau^2 = \tfrac{27}{800}$ (Example~\ref{ex:boundary}).
(ii)~\emph{The minimum-variance end, locally.} If the bridge reaches
minimum variance exactly at $\gamma = 1$, then $V_0'(1) = 0$ automatically
and $V_0''(1) \ge 0$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:mv}); if $V_0''(1) > 0$ and the
marginal noise sensitivity $G'(1)$ is positive, every sufficiently small
noise level pushes the optimizer into the interior, by
$1 - \gamma^*(\tau) \sim \tau^2 G'(1)/V_0''(1)$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:mvlocal}). The sign condition is essential.
(iii)~\emph{An exactly solved family.} For an exchangeable four-asset
family with common cross-block noise, the expected out-of-sample variance is
computed in closed form; it is strictly convex on $[0,1]$, its unique
minimizer $\gamma^*(e)$ is interior for every admissible noise amplitude
$e \in (0, c]$, and it decreases monotonically from $\gamma^*(0) = 1$ to
$\gamma^*(c) = \tfrac12$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:family}). This is a statement
about the exact objective, not a surrogate. One care is required: at large
noise the raw recursion has a removable singularity at one interior
coupling, so the objective is defined by continuous extension, and the
minimizer is proved to sit strictly inside the region where the recursion
is defined. A companion exact example with
noise in a single covariance entry (Example~\ref{ex:interior}) shows that
interior optimality survives without the common-noise symmetry. A clipped
monotone frontier for the second-order surrogate
(Theorem~\ref{thm:frontier}) organizes the general case. We are explicit
about what is not claimed: covariance error does not always force an
interior optimum, the noise cost is not $O(\gamma^2\tau^2)$ in general, and
either endpoint can be optimal on a nontrivial interval of noise levels.

\section{The recursion}
\label{sec:setup}

\emph{The family.} Following \cite{cotton2024schur}, fix an asset order and a
balanced bisection tree; assume for notational simplicity that $n$ is a power
of two, so sibling blocks have equal size (a step-up matrix enters in the
general case \cite{cotton2024schur}). A hierarchical allocation assigns a node's
capital to its children $(L, R)$ in the ratio $1/\nu(\cdot_L) : 1/\nu(\cdot_R)$
for an inverse-fitness functional $\nu$, and recurses. Writing a node's block
of the covariance as
$\left(\begin{smallmatrix} A & B \\ B^\top & D \end{smallmatrix}\right)$,
hierarchical risk parity passes $A$ and $D$ to the children unchanged. The
Schur-complementary family instead passes \emph{augmented} blocks built from
the $\gamma$-damped Schur complement and its companion vector,
\begin{equation}
  A^c(\gamma) \;=\; A - \gamma\, B D^{-1} B^\top,
  \qquad
  b_A(\gamma) \;=\; \mathbf{1} - \gamma\, B D^{-1} \mathbf{1},
  \label{eq:family}
\end{equation}
and symmetrically for $D^c(\gamma), b_D(\gamma)$. In the canonical
presentation the intra-group matrix is $A^c(\gamma)$ with an elementwise
division by $b_A b_A^\top$ and the inter-group fitness is evaluated on
$\big(A^c(\gamma)^{-1} \circ b_A b_A^\top\big)^{-1}$. At $\gamma = 0$ every
member is HRP. At $\gamma = 1$ the scheme replicates the minimum-variance
portfolio; the one-step form of that equivalence is
Proposition~\ref{prop:minvar}, following Appendix~A of
\cite{cotton2024schur}.

\begin{proposition}[$\gamma = 1$ replicates minimum variance]
\label{prop:minvar}
Let $\Sigma \succ 0$ have blocks $A, B, D$ as above and write
$A^c = A - BD^{-1}B^\top$, $b_A = \mathbf{1} - BD^{-1}\mathbf{1}$, and
symmetrically $D^c, b_D$. Then
\begin{equation}
  \Sigma^{-1}\mathbf{1}
  \;=\;
  \begin{pmatrix} (A^c)^{-1} b_A \\ (D^c)^{-1} b_D \end{pmatrix}.
  \label{eq:blockinv}
\end{equation}
For any SPD $Q$ and $b \ne 0$, the constrained problem
$w(Q,b) = \argminop_{w :\, b^\top w = 1} w^\top Q w$ has solution and
attained variance
\begin{equation}
  w(Q, b) \;=\; \frac{Q^{-1}b}{b^\top Q^{-1} b},
  \qquad
  \nu(Q, b) \;=\; w(Q,b)^\top Q\, w(Q,b) \;=\; \frac{1}{b^\top Q^{-1} b},
  \label{eq:constrained}
\end{equation}
so that $Q^{-1}b = w(Q,b)/\nu(Q,b)$. Consequently the two-block scheme that
allocates between blocks in the ratio $1/\nu(A^c,b_A) : 1/\nu(D^c,b_D)$ and
within blocks by $w(A^c,b_A)$, $w(D^c,b_D)$ reproduces
$w \propto \Sigma^{-1}\mathbf{1}$, and recursive application to the
sub-problems (each again of the form $Q^{-1}b$) extends this down the tree.
If a block has $b_A = 0$ then by \eqref{eq:blockinv} that block of
$\Sigma^{-1}\mathbf{1}$ is exactly zero and the block receives nothing; the
normalized $w(Q,b)$ is simply not formed. (For instance
$\Sigma = \left(\begin{smallmatrix}2&1\\1&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ has
$b_A = 0$ and $\Sigma^{-1}\mathbf{1} = (0, 1)^\top$.)
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For \eqref{eq:blockinv}, multiply the block inversion identity
\[
  \begin{pmatrix} A & B \\ B^\top & D \end{pmatrix}^{-1}
  =
  \begin{pmatrix} (A^c)^{-1} & 0 \\ 0 & (D^c)^{-1} \end{pmatrix}
  \begin{pmatrix} I & -BD^{-1} \\ -B^\top A^{-1} & I \end{pmatrix}
\]
on the right by $\mathbf{1}$; the top block is
$(A^c)^{-1}(\mathbf{1} - BD^{-1}\mathbf{1}) = (A^c)^{-1}b_A$ and the bottom
block is analogous. For \eqref{eq:constrained}, the Lagrangian condition is
$Qw = \lambda b$, so $w = \lambda Q^{-1}b$; the constraint $b^\top w = 1$
forces $\lambda = 1/(b^\top Q^{-1}b)$, giving the stated $w(Q,b)$, and
substituting back gives $\nu(Q,b) = \lambda$. Rearranging,
$Q^{-1}b = w(Q,b)\,(b^\top Q^{-1}b) = w(Q,b)/\nu(Q,b)$. Apply this to each
block of \eqref{eq:blockinv}: the unnormalized block solutions are the
normalized sub-portfolios scaled by exactly the inverse fitness $1/\nu$,
which is the stated two-block scheme.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Minimum variance in disguise]
\label{rem:disguise}
The constrained problem \eqref{eq:constrained} was stated for general
$b$, and that generality is not idle. Several portfolio problems that look
different are solutions of $Q^{-1}b$ for some $(Q, b)$ and are therefore
minimum variance in disguise: the tangency portfolio ($b = \mu$), maximum
diversification ($b = \sigma$, the vector of volatilities), minimum
tracking error against a benchmark (minimum variance in active weights),
beta hedging ($b$ a vector of exposures), and the variance-optimal
combination of forecasts, where $Q$ is the covariance of forecast errors
and $b = \mathbf{1}$ \cite{bates1969}. The block identity
\eqref{eq:blockinv} and the damped bridge apply to each with $\mathbf{1}$
replaced by $b$ throughout. The noise calculus of this paper is developed
for $b = \mathbf{1}$; we expect, but do not prove here, that the endpoint
results carry over with the companion vectors $b_A(\gamma)$ built from $b$.
\end{remark}

\emph{The variant analyzed.} Appendix~A of \cite{cotton2024schur} licenses an
equivalent ``collapse'' of the pair $(A^c, b_A)$ into a single matrix
$\widetilde A$ with $\widetilde A^{-1}\mathbf{1} = A^c(\gamma)^{-1}
b_A(\gamma)$. The reference implementations take exactly this
route.\footnote{\texttt{skfolio}'s \texttt{SchurComplementary} and the
\texttt{allocation} package use identical augmentation and fitness routines;
see \texttt{github.com/skfolio/skfolio} and
\texttt{github.com/microprediction/allocation}.} It is the recursion we
analyze. Each child block is replaced by
\begin{equation}
  A_\gamma \;=\; \operatorname{sym}\!\Big[\big(I - \gamma B D^{-1}\big)^{-1}
  A^c(\gamma)\Big],
  \qquad
  D_\gamma \;=\; \big(A \leftrightarrow D,\ B \to B^\top\big),
  \label{eq:aug}
\end{equation}
where $\operatorname{sym}(X) = \tfrac12(X + X^\top)$; note
$\big(I - \gamma BD^{-1}\big)\mathbf{1} = b_A(\gamma)$, so \eqref{eq:aug} is
the collapse of \eqref{eq:family}, up to the symmetrization. The fitness is
the \emph{naive portfolio variance}
\begin{equation}
  \nu(X) \;=\; \tilde w^\top X\, \tilde w,
  \qquad
  \tilde w_i \;=\; \frac{1/X_{ii}}{\sum_j 1/X_{jj}},
  \label{eq:naive}
\end{equation}
so the left child receives the fraction
$\nu(D_\gamma)/\big(\nu(A_\gamma) + \nu(D_\gamma)\big)$ of the node's
capital, and the recursion continues inside each augmented block down to
singletons. Nodes with a singleton child perform no augmentation; the
implementations skip the augmentation whenever \emph{either} child has one
asset, so a two-asset node splits by the inverse of its already augmented
diagonal entries. At $\gamma = 0$ the augmentation \eqref{eq:aug} is the
identity and the recursion is exactly HRP over the fixed order.
Implementations optionally cap $\gamma$ at an in-sample variance turning
point (\texttt{keep\_monotonic}); we analyze the raw recursion. One thing
cannot be simplified away. The inter-group fitness must be a quadratic form
evaluated on the \emph{augmented} block. A diagonal-only $\nu$, as used in
some HRP variants, generally does not preserve the Schur block-inversion
identity \eqref{eq:blockinv} that exact minimum-variance recovery requires.

Two structural facts hold across the entire family and not just the variant
\eqref{eq:aug}: by \eqref{eq:family} the cross-block $B$ enters every member
only through the products $\gamma B D^{-1}(\cdot)$, and at $\gamma = 0$ the
augmentation is the identity with $b_A = \mathbf{1}$.

\emph{The noise model.} Let $\Sigma \succ 0$ be the population covariance
and let the estimate be
\begin{equation}
  \widehat\Sigma_\tau \;=\; \Sigma + \tau N,
  \label{eq:noise}
\end{equation}
where $N$ is a random symmetric matrix. The objects of study are the
expected out-of-sample variance and its population limit,
\begin{equation}
  F(\gamma; \tau) \;=\; \E\big[\, w(\gamma; \widehat\Sigma_\tau)^\top \Sigma\,
  w(\gamma; \widehat\Sigma_\tau) \,\big],
  \qquad
  V_0(\gamma) \;=\; F(\gamma; 0),
  \label{eq:F}
\end{equation}
where $w(\gamma; M)$ denotes the allocation the recursion produces from an
input matrix $M$. The allocation $w$ and the realized variance
$w^\top \Sigma w$ are rational functions of $(\gamma, M)$, hence $C^\infty$
wherever their denominators do not vanish. When $N$ has finite support (as
in all examples below) $F$ is itself a rational function of $\gamma$; for
general $N$ it is merely smooth, under the assumptions we now state.

\begin{assumption}
\label{ass:reg}
There are $\bar\gamma \in (0,1]$, $\bar\tau > 0$, and a compact set
$K = [0,\bar\gamma] \times \mathcal{M}$, with $\mathcal{M}$ a compact
neighborhood of $\Sigma$ in the symmetric matrices, such that every
denominator appearing in the recursion (the inverses in \eqref{eq:aug}, the
diagonal entries in \eqref{eq:naive}, and the fitness sums) is bounded away
from zero on $K$; and $N$ is bounded with
$\Sigma + \tau N \in \mathcal{M}$ for all $|\tau| \le \bar\tau$.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}
\label{ass:noise}
$N$ is supported on the top-level cross-block ($N_{LL} = 0$, $N_{RR} = 0$)
and its law is symmetric: $N \overset{d}{=} -N$.
\end{assumption}

Under Assumption~\ref{ass:reg}, $(\gamma, \tau) \mapsto
w(\gamma; \Sigma + \tau N)$ is $C^\infty$ on $[0,\bar\gamma] \times
[-\bar\tau, \bar\tau]$ for every realization, with all derivatives bounded
uniformly over realizations; $F$ is then $C^\infty$ by differentiation under
the expectation (the derivatives are dominated by their uniform bounds).
Assumption~\ref{ass:noise} makes $F$ even in $\tau$.

\begin{remark}[Model scope]
\label{rem:scope-model}
The model \eqref{eq:noise}--\eqref{eq:F} with Assumption~\ref{ass:noise} is
stylized on purpose: it isolates the cross-block channel, which is the only
channel the coupling $\gamma$ touches. A natural generative alternative is
partial information about $\Sigma$ itself, for instance $\widehat\Sigma$
equal to the sample covariance of $T$ independent draws from
$\mathcal{N}(0, \Sigma)$, which corresponds to $\tau \asymp T^{-1/2}$ with
noise supported on \emph{every} block. Under whole-matrix noise the exact
invariance of Lemma~\ref{lem:blind} applies only to the cross-block
component of the noise: $F(0;\tau)$ is then itself noisy, and the endpoint
comparison becomes a comparison of noise costs rather than a comparison
against a noise-free anchor. The analysis of that model is not attempted
here; the results below are exact for \eqref{eq:noise} under
Assumptions~\ref{ass:reg}--\ref{ass:noise} and are offered as the
cross-block part of the larger question.
\end{remark}

\section{The hierarchical end}
\label{sec:endpoints}

\begin{lemma}[The HRP end is blind to the cross-block]
\label{lem:blind}
For a fixed tree, $w(0; M)$ is a function of the diagonal blocks $(A, D)$
alone. Consequently, under Assumption~\ref{ass:noise},
$w(0; \widehat\Sigma_\tau) = w(0; \Sigma)$ almost surely and
$F(0; \tau) = V_0(0)$ exactly, for every $\tau$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
At $\gamma = 0$ the augmentation \eqref{eq:aug} returns its first argument.
The top-level split therefore uses $\nu(A)$ and $\nu(D)$, and
\eqref{eq:naive} reads only entries of $A$ and of $D$. The recursion then
continues inside the unmodified blocks $A$ and $D$; every quantity computed
below the top level is a function of entries of $A$ or entries of $D$. The
top-level cross-block $B$ appears nowhere. (Cross-blocks interior to $A$ or
$D$ \emph{are} read, through the naive variances of their parents; only the
top-level $B$ is never read.) The second sentence follows because
$\widehat\Sigma_\tau$ and $\Sigma$ share their diagonal blocks under
Assumption~\ref{ass:noise}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Scope]
\label{rem:scope}
The lemma concerns a fixed tree and this particular variant: the recursion
reads the naive variance of full child sub-blocks, as in
\cite{lopezdeprado2016}. A split on diagonal variances alone would read
nothing off-diagonal. When the tree itself is data-driven (a dendrogram, or
Fiedler seriation \cite{cotton2024schur}), the ordering step does read the
cross-block, but only through the discrete tree choice, which for a generic
estimate is locally constant; the differential statements below are
unaffected for noise small enough to leave the tree unchanged. (In
Example~\ref{ex:interior} the seriation returns the same top partition on
both noise branches, so fixing the order there is without loss.)
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[Uniform separation from HRP under small noise]
\label{thm:nohrp}
Suppose Assumption~\ref{ass:reg} holds, $N$ is bounded (no support or
symmetry restriction needed), and $V_0'(0) < 0$, i.e.\ coupling has
first-order population value. Then there exist $\varepsilon > 0$,
$\tau_0 > 0$, and $c > 0$ such that
\[
  \partial_\gamma F(\gamma; \tau) \;\le\; -c
  \qquad\text{for all } 0 \le \gamma \le \varepsilon,\ 0 \le \tau \le \tau_0.
\]
Consequently every minimizer of $F(\cdot\,;\tau)$ over $[0,\bar\gamma]$ lies
in $[\varepsilon, \bar\gamma]$, for every $\tau \le \tau_0$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Write $V(\gamma; M) = w(\gamma; M)^\top \Sigma\, w(\gamma; M)$. Under
Assumption~\ref{ass:reg}, $\partial_\gamma V$ exists, is continuous, and is
bounded on $K$, so
$\partial_\gamma F(\gamma;\tau) = \E[\partial_\gamma V(\gamma; \Sigma +
\tau N)]$ by differentiation under the expectation, and
$(\gamma, \tau) \mapsto \partial_\gamma F(\gamma;\tau)$ is jointly
continuous on $[0,\bar\gamma] \times [0,\bar\tau]$ by dominated convergence.
Its value at $(0, 0)$ is $V_0'(0) < 0$. By continuity there is a relatively
open neighborhood of $(0,0)$ on which
$\partial_\gamma F \le \tfrac12 V_0'(0) =: -c < 0$; choose
$\varepsilon, \tau_0$ so that
$[0,\varepsilon] \times [0,\tau_0]$ sits inside it. For $\tau \le \tau_0$
and any $\gamma \in [0, \varepsilon)$, integrating the derivative gives
$F(\varepsilon;\tau) \le F(\gamma;\tau) - c(\varepsilon - \gamma) <
F(\gamma;\tau)$, so no point of $[0, \varepsilon)$ can be a minimizer.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The size of the noise cost}
\label{sec:noisecost}

How large is $F - V_0$ near the HRP end? The correct answer is order
$\gamma\tau^2$, and it is worth displaying the mechanism, because a
plausible shortcut gives the wrong exponent. Write the estimated cross-block
as $\widehat B = B + \tau E$ for a realization $E$ of $N$'s cross-block.
Expanding \eqref{eq:aug} at small $\gamma$,
\begin{equation}
  A_\gamma(\widehat B)
  \;=\; A + \gamma\,\operatorname{sym}\!\big(\widehat B D^{-1} A
        - \widehat B D^{-1} \widehat B^\top\big) + O(\gamma^2).
  \label{eq:augexp}
\end{equation}
Averaging over the symmetric branches $\widehat B = B \pm \tau E$, the terms
linear in $\tau$ cancel, but the term quadratic in $\widehat B$ does not:
\begin{equation}
  \tfrac12 \sum_{\pm} A_\gamma(B \pm \tau E) \;-\; A_\gamma(B)
  \;=\; -\,\gamma\,\tau^2\, E D^{-1} E^\top \;+\; O(\gamma^2 \tau^2).
  \label{eq:avgshift}
\end{equation}
The expected augmented block is displaced at order $\gamma\tau^2$, and the
allocation inherits the displacement unless the weight functional happens to
annihilate it. In terms of derivatives of the realized variance,
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\partial^2}{\partial \tau^2}\big(w^\top \Sigma w\big)
  \;=\; 2\, w_\tau^\top \Sigma\, w_\tau \;+\; 2\, w_{\tau\tau}^\top \Sigma\, w,
  \label{eq:secondder}
\end{equation}
and while $w_\tau = O(\gamma)$ (each appearance of $\widehat B$ in
\eqref{eq:aug} carries a factor $\gamma$), the second derivative
$w_{\tau\tau}$ is only $O(\gamma)$ as well, through the quadratic term
visible in \eqref{eq:avgshift}. The first term of \eqref{eq:secondder} is
$O(\gamma^2)$; the second is generically $O(\gamma)$. The shortcut
``$w_\tau = O(\gamma)$, therefore $F - V_0 = O(\gamma^2\tau^2)$'' bounds
only the first term and is false; Example~\ref{ex:boundary} makes the
failure exact.

\begin{theorem}[Noise cost near the HRP end]
\label{thm:noisecost}
Under Assumptions~\ref{ass:reg} and~\ref{ass:noise} there is a continuous
bounded function $H$ on $[0,\bar\gamma] \times [0,\bar\tau]$ with
\begin{equation}
  F(\gamma;\tau) - V_0(\gamma) \;=\; \gamma\, \tau^2\, H(\gamma, \tau),
  \qquad\text{hence}\qquad
  \big|F(\gamma;\tau) - V_0(\gamma)\big| \;\le\; C\,\gamma\,\tau^2
  \label{eq:gt2}
\end{equation}
with $C = \sup |H|$. The exponent of $\gamma$ cannot be improved: in
Example~\ref{ex:boundary}, $H(0,0) = \tfrac{8}{189} \neq 0$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let $\Phi(\gamma, \tau) = F(\gamma;\tau) - V_0(\gamma)$, which is $C^\infty$
on the compact rectangle $R = [0,\bar\gamma] \times [-\bar\tau, \bar\tau]$
by Assumption~\ref{ass:reg}. Three exact identities hold on $R$:
$\Phi(0, \tau) = 0$ for all $\tau$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:blind});
$\Phi(\gamma, 0) = 0$ for all $\gamma$ (no noise); and
$\Phi(\gamma, -\tau) = \Phi(\gamma, \tau)$ (Assumption~\ref{ass:noise}).
Fix $\gamma$ and Taylor-expand in $\tau$ with integral remainder: since
$\Phi(\gamma, 0) = 0$ and, by evenness, $\partial_\tau \Phi(\gamma, 0) = 0$,
\[
  \Phi(\gamma, \tau)
  \;=\; \tau^2 \int_0^1 (1-s)\, \partial^2_{\tau\tau}
        \Phi(\gamma, s\tau)\, ds
  \;=:\; \tau^2\, \Psi(\gamma, \tau).
\]
$\Psi$ is $C^1$ on $R$ (differentiation under the integral, with the third
derivatives of $\Phi$ bounded on $R$). Differentiating the identity
$\Phi(0,\tau) = 0$ twice in $\tau$ gives
$\partial^2_{\tau\tau}\Phi(0, \tau) = 0$ for all $\tau$, hence
$\Psi(0, \tau) = 0$. Taylor in $\gamma$ with integral remainder then gives
\[
  \Psi(\gamma, \tau) \;=\; \gamma \int_0^1
  \partial_\gamma \Psi(u\gamma, \tau)\, du \;=:\; \gamma\, H(\gamma, \tau),
\]
with $H$ continuous and bounded on $R$ by the same uniform derivative
bounds. Combining the two displays yields \eqref{eq:gt2}. The sharpness
claim is Example~\ref{ex:boundary}, where
$\partial_\gamma(F - V_0)(0; \tau) = \tfrac{8}{189}\tau^2$ exactly.
\end{proof}

With $C^4$ regularity in $\tau$ (available under Assumption~\ref{ass:reg}),
the same identities give the uniform expansion
\begin{equation}
  F(\gamma;\tau) \;=\; V_0(\gamma) + \tau^2 G(\gamma) + O(\tau^4),
  \qquad
  G(\gamma) \;=\; \tfrac12\,\partial^2_{\tau\tau} F(\gamma; 0)
  \;=\; \gamma\, h(\gamma),
  \label{eq:expansion}
\end{equation}
with $h$ continuous, so $G(0) = 0$, but in general
$G'(0) = h(0) \ne 0$: $G$ vanishes only linearly at the HRP end. The exact
boundary derivative is
\begin{equation}
  \partial_\gamma F(0;\tau) \;=\; V_0'(0) + \tau^2\, H(0, \tau).
  \label{eq:boundarycalc}
\end{equation}

\begin{remark}[When is the stronger $\gamma^2\tau^2$ bound valid?]
\label{rem:gamma2}
The bound $|F - V_0| \le C\gamma^2\tau^2$ is recoverable under one
additional, substantive hypothesis: that the first coupling variation is
unbiased under the noise, i.e.\
$\partial_\gamma F(0;\tau) = V_0'(0)$ for all small $\tau$. Then both the
value and the first $\gamma$-derivative of $F - V_0$ vanish at $\gamma = 0$
and a third application of the Taylor argument yields
$F - V_0 = \gamma^2\tau^2 \widetilde H$. The recursion does not satisfy this
generically, because
\[
  \E\big[(B + \tau N_B)\, D^{-1} (B + \tau N_B)^\top\big]
  \;=\; B D^{-1} B^\top + \tau^2\, \E\big[N_B D^{-1} N_B^\top\big],
\]
and that quadratic bias enters \eqref{eq:aug} multiplied by a single power
of $\gamma$. Both exact examples in this paper happen to possess a further
downstream cancellation (Remark~\ref{rem:cancel}), which is why numerical
evidence from them alone can suggest, wrongly, that the quadratic bound is
general.
\end{remark}

\begin{example}[HRP locally optimal at a finite noise level]
\label{ex:boundary}
Let $n = 4$ with
\[
  A = D = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -\tfrac12 \\ -\tfrac12 & 2 \end{pmatrix},
  \qquad
  B = \tfrac{1}{10}\,\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^\top,
  \qquad
  E = e_1 e_1^\top,
  \qquad
  \widehat B = B \pm \tau E \ \ \text{w.p. } \tfrac12 \text{ each},
\]
and $\Sigma = \left(\begin{smallmatrix} A & B \\ B & A
\end{smallmatrix}\right)$, which is SPD (both $A + B$ and $A - B$ are
positive definite). Then, exactly and for every $\tau$,
\begin{equation}
  \partial_\gamma F(0; \tau) \;=\; -\frac{1}{700} + \frac{8}{189}\,\tau^2 .
  \label{eq:boundaryderiv}
\end{equation}
In particular $V_0'(0) = -\tfrac{1}{700} < 0$, so the premise of
Theorem~\ref{thm:nohrp} holds, yet $\partial_\gamma F(0;\tau) > 0$ as soon
as $\tau^2 > \tfrac{27}{800}$, i.e.\ $\tau > \sqrt{27/800} \approx 0.1837$:
the HRP endpoint is then a one-sided local minimum. Moreover
$F(\gamma;\tau) - V_0(\gamma) = \tfrac{8}{189}\gamma\tau^2 + O(\gamma^2)$,
so no bound of the form $C\gamma^2\tau^2$ can hold on any neighborhood of
$\gamma = 0$.
\end{example}
\begin{proof}
The computation is elementary and worth walking through. \emph{Step 1
(symmetry).} Since $A = D$ and each realized $\widehat B$ is symmetric, the
two augmented blocks in \eqref{eq:aug} coincide, so the top-level split is
$\tfrac12 : \tfrac12$ for every $\gamma$, $\tau$, and branch, and
$w = \tfrac12(u; u)$ where $u$ are the inverse-diagonal weights
\eqref{eq:naive} of the common augmented block $A_\gamma(\widehat B)$.
\emph{Step 2 (objective).} With the true $\Sigma$,
\[
  V \;=\; w^\top \Sigma w
  \;=\; \tfrac14\big(u^\top A u + 2\,u^\top B u + u^\top A u\big)
  \;=\; \tfrac12\, u^\top (A + B)\, u .
\]
\emph{Step 3 (first-order augmentation).} By \eqref{eq:augexp} with
$D = A$ and $\widehat B$ symmetric,
$A_\gamma(\widehat B) = A + \gamma H(\widehat B) + O(\gamma^2)$ with
$H(\widehat B) = \widehat B - \widehat B A^{-1} \widehat B$; the
symmetrization does not alter this since $H(\widehat B)$ is symmetric.
\emph{Step 4 (weight derivative).} The weights \eqref{eq:naive} depend only
on the diagonal. With $\operatorname{diag}(A) = (1, 2)$,
$u(0) = (\tfrac23, \tfrac13)$, and differentiating
$u(\gamma) = \big(A_{22} + \gamma H_{22},\, A_{11} + \gamma H_{11}\big) /
\big(3 + \gamma(H_{11} + H_{22})\big)$ at $\gamma = 0$ gives
\[
  u'(0) \;=\; \frac{H_{22} - 2 H_{11}}{9} \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ -1
  \end{pmatrix}.
\]
\emph{Step 5 (variance derivative).} With $M = A + B$ one computes
$M (1, -1)^\top = (\tfrac{3}{2}, -\tfrac{5}{2})^\top$ and
$u(0)^\top M (1,-1)^\top = \tfrac{2}{3}\cdot\tfrac32 - \tfrac13\cdot\tfrac52
= \tfrac16$, so
\[
  \partial_\gamma V\big|_{\gamma=0}
  \;=\; u(0)^\top M\, u'(0)
  \;=\; \frac{H_{22} - 2H_{11}}{54}.
\]
\emph{Step 6 (average over branches).} $H$ is quadratic in $\widehat B$, so
averaging is exact:
\[
  \tfrac12\sum_{\pm} H(B \pm \tau E)
  \;=\; B - B A^{-1} B - \tau^2\, E A^{-1} E .
\]
With $A^{-1} = \tfrac17\left(\begin{smallmatrix} 8 & 2 \\ 2 & 4
\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ one gets
$B A^{-1} B = \tfrac{4}{175}\,\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^\top$ and
$E A^{-1} E = \tfrac{8}{7}\, e_1 e_1^\top$, so the averaged $H$ has
$\bar H_{11} = \tfrac{1}{10} - \tfrac{4}{175} - \tfrac{8}{7}\tau^2$ and
$\bar H_{22} = \tfrac{1}{10} - \tfrac{4}{175}$. Substituting into Step 5,
\[
  \partial_\gamma F(0;\tau)
  \;=\; \frac{\bar H_{22} - 2 \bar H_{11}}{54}
  \;=\; \frac{-\tfrac{27}{350} + \tfrac{16}{7}\tau^2}{54}
  \;=\; -\frac{1}{700} + \frac{8}{189}\,\tau^2 .
\]
Since $F$ is smooth in $\gamma$ near $0$ (denominators reduce to those of
$A$ at $\gamma = 0$), the exchange of expectation and derivative is
justified by the finite support of the noise. The certificate script
confirms \eqref{eq:boundaryderiv} in exact rational arithmetic, by
dual-number (first-order jet) evaluation of the full recursion, at
$\tau \in \{0, \tfrac{1}{10}, \tfrac{3}{20}, \tfrac15, \tfrac{3}{10}\}$:
for instance $\partial_\gamma F(0; \tfrac{3}{20}) = -\tfrac{1}{2100}$ and
$\partial_\gamma F(0; \tfrac15) = +\tfrac{1}{3780}$.
\end{proof}

\section{The minimum-variance end}
\label{sec:mvend}

Suppose the bridge under study reaches minimum variance exactly at
$\gamma = 1$: writing
$w_* = \Sigma^{-1}\mathbf{1}/(\mathbf{1}^\top\Sigma^{-1}\mathbf{1})$,
suppose $w(1;\Sigma) = w_*$ while $\mathbf{1}^\top w(\gamma;\Sigma) = 1$ for
all $\gamma$. This holds for the canonical member of the family
(Proposition~\ref{prop:minvar}) and, in the solved family of
Section~\ref{sec:family}, for the implemented variant as well; it is not
automatic for the implemented variant in general.

\begin{lemma}[Flatness at an exact minimum-variance endpoint]
\label{lem:mv}
Under the above, $V_0$ is differentiable at $\gamma = 1$ with
\[
  V_0'(1) \;=\; 0,
  \qquad
  V_0''(1) \;=\; 2\, w_\gamma(1)^\top \Sigma\, w_\gamma(1) \;\ge\; 0,
\]
where $w_\gamma(1) = \partial_\gamma w(\gamma;\Sigma)|_{\gamma=1}$; the
second derivative is strictly positive whenever the bridge arrives with a
nonzero tangent, $w_\gamma(1) \ne 0$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Since $\Sigma w_* = \lambda \mathbf{1}$ with
$\lambda = 1/(\mathbf{1}^\top\Sigma^{-1}\mathbf{1})$, differentiating
$V_0(\gamma) = w(\gamma)^\top\Sigma\,w(\gamma)$ at $\gamma = 1$ gives
$V_0'(1) = 2\, w_\gamma(1)^\top \Sigma w_* = 2\lambda\,
w_\gamma(1)^\top \mathbf{1} = 0$, because $\mathbf{1}^\top w(\gamma) \equiv 1$
implies $\mathbf{1}^\top w_\gamma \equiv 0$. Differentiating once more,
$V_0''(1) = 2 w_\gamma(1)^\top \Sigma w_\gamma(1) +
2 w_{\gamma\gamma}(1)^\top \Sigma w_*$, and the second term vanishes for the
same reason ($\mathbf{1}^\top w_{\gamma\gamma} \equiv 0$).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Small noise moves the optimum inside]
\label{thm:mvlocal}
Assume the setting of Lemma~\ref{lem:mv} with $V_0''(1) > 0$, the expansion
\eqref{eq:expansion} valid in $C^2$ uniformly near $\gamma = 1$, and
$G'(1) > 0$. Then there are $\tau_1 > 0$ and a $C^1$ curve
$\gamma^*(\tau)$, defined for $0 \le \tau < \tau_1$, of local minimizers of
$F(\cdot\,;\tau)$ with
\begin{equation}
  \gamma^*(\tau) \;=\; 1 - \frac{G'(1)}{V_0''(1)}\,\tau^2 + O(\tau^4)
  \;<\; 1 \quad (\tau > 0).
  \label{eq:mvshift}
\end{equation}
If moreover $\gamma = 1$ is the unique global minimizer of $V_0$ on
$[0,\bar\gamma]$, then for all sufficiently small $\tau$ the curve gives
the unique global minimizer of $F(\cdot\,;\tau)$. The sign condition is essential: if $G'(1) < 0$, full
coupling remains locally optimal for small noise.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let $g(\gamma, s) = \partial_\gamma F(\gamma; \sqrt{s})$ for $s = \tau^2 \ge
0$; by \eqref{eq:expansion} in $C^2$, $g(\gamma, s) = V_0'(\gamma) +
s\,G'(\gamma) + O(s^2)$ with the error uniform in $\gamma$ near $1$, and $g$
is $C^1$ in $(\gamma, s)$ there. By Lemma~\ref{lem:mv}, $g(1, 0) =
V_0'(1) = 0$ and $\partial_\gamma g(1,0) = V_0''(1) > 0$. The implicit
function theorem gives a $C^1$ curve $\gamma^*(s)$ with
$g(\gamma^*(s), s) = 0$, $\gamma^*(0) = 1$, and
\[
  \frac{d\gamma^*}{ds}\Big|_{s=0}
  \;=\; -\,\frac{\partial_s g(1,0)}{\partial_\gamma g(1,0)}
  \;=\; -\,\frac{G'(1)}{V_0''(1)} \;<\; 0,
\]
which is \eqref{eq:mvshift}. Since $\partial_\gamma g > 0$ near $(1,0)$,
each $\gamma^*(s)$ is a strict local minimizer. For global optimality:
$F(\cdot\,;\tau) \to V_0$ uniformly on $[0,\bar\gamma]$ as $\tau \to 0$
(by \eqref{eq:gt2} or directly by uniform continuity), so if $\gamma = 1$
is the unique global minimizer of $V_0$, minimizers of $F$ must enter any
prescribed neighborhood of $1$ for small $\tau$ (outside such a
neighborhood $V_0$ exceeds $V_0(1)$ by a positive margin, by compactness),
where strict convexity of $F$ in
$\gamma$ (from $\partial_\gamma g > 0$) makes $\gamma^*(s)$ the only
candidate. If $G'(1) < 0$ the same computation gives
$d\gamma^*/ds > 0$: the unconstrained stationary point exits the interval
at $\gamma = 1$ and full coupling is locally optimal.
\end{proof}

\section{An exactly solved family}
\label{sec:family}

Fix the four-asset covariance
\begin{equation}
  \Sigma \;=\;
  \begin{pmatrix}
    1 & \tfrac12 & \tfrac35 & \tfrac35 \\
    \tfrac12 & 1 & \tfrac35 & \tfrac35 \\
    \tfrac35 & \tfrac35 & 4 & 2 \\
    \tfrac35 & \tfrac35 & 2 & 4
  \end{pmatrix},
  \label{eq:familycov}
\end{equation}
i.e.\ blocks $\{1,2\}$ and $\{3,4\}$ with unit and double volatilities,
within-block correlation $\tfrac12$, and cross-block correlation
$\rho_c = \tfrac{3}{10}$. Let the noise perturb the cross-block correlation
\emph{in common}: all four cross entries move together,
$\rho_c \to \rho_c \pm \tau$ with probability $\tfrac12$ each. At the level
of the two blocks' equal-weight portfolios, the population variances and
cross covariance are
\[
  a = \tfrac34, \qquad d = 3, \qquad c = \tfrac35,
  \qquad S := a + d = \tfrac{15}{4}, \qquad K := S - 2c = \tfrac{51}{20},
\]
and the estimated group-level cross covariance is $Z = c \pm e$ with
$e = 2\tau$ (the blocks have volatilities $1$ and $2$).

\begin{lemma}[Exact reduction to a scalar bridge]
\label{lem:reduce}
Assume $a < d$ and $4c^2 < ad$, as in \eqref{eq:familycov}, and fix
$\gamma \in [0,1]$ and a realized $z = c \pm e$ with $0 \le z \le 2c$. The
augmentation \eqref{eq:aug} of the $D$ block requires the factor
$I - \gamma B^\top A^{-1}$ to be invertible, and that holds precisely when
$\gamma z \ne a$; the $A$-side factor $I - \gamma B D^{-1}$ is invertible
for every such $(\gamma, z)$, since $\gamma z \le 2c < d$. Wherever the
recursion is defined, its within-block weights are exactly
$(\tfrac12, \tfrac12)$ in each block, the augmented fitnesses are
\begin{equation}
  \nu_{A_\gamma}(z) \;=\; \frac{ad - \gamma z^2}{d - \gamma z},
  \qquad
  \nu_{D_\gamma}(z) \;=\; \frac{ad - \gamma z^2}{a - \gamma z},
  \label{eq:nuscalar}
\end{equation}
and the left-block weight is
\begin{equation}
  x_\gamma(z) \;=\; \frac{d - \gamma z}{a + d - 2\gamma z},
  \label{eq:xgz}
\end{equation}
with realized out-of-sample variance $Q(x_\gamma(z))$, where
$Q(x) = a x^2 + d(1-x)^2 + 2c\,x(1-x)$. The formula \eqref{eq:xgz} is a
rational function of $(\gamma, z)$ whose denominator obeys
$S - 2\gamma z \ge S - 4c > 0$. It therefore extends the recursion's group
weight continuously through the point $\gamma z = a$, where the extension
gives the $D$ block the weight
$1 - x_\gamma(z) = (a - \gamma z)/(S - 2\gamma z) = 0$. This is the analog
of the $b = 0$ convention in Proposition~\ref{prop:minvar}: a block whose
augmented fitness diverges receives nothing. We call the allocation defined
by \eqref{eq:xgz} on all of $[0,1]$ the \emph{extended} allocation and
write $F_{\mathrm{ext}}$ for its expected out-of-sample variance. The
singular point exists in $[0,1]$ only on the upper noise branch and only
when $z = c + e \ge a$, that is, when $e \ge a - c$; for
\eqref{eq:familycov} that threshold is $a - c = \tfrac{3}{20}$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write the asset-level blocks as $A_2 = \left(\begin{smallmatrix} 1 & \frac12
\\ \frac12 & 1 \end{smallmatrix}\right)$, $D_2 = \left(\begin{smallmatrix}
4 & 2 \\ 2 & 4\end{smallmatrix}\right)$, and $\widehat B = z\,
\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^\top$ (each cross entry equals
$1 \cdot 2 \cdot (\rho_c \pm \tau) = z$). All three matrices are
exchangeable (invariant under swapping the two assets of a block), and the
augmentation \eqref{eq:aug} preserves exchangeability, so each augmented
block has equal diagonal entries and \eqref{eq:naive} gives
$(\tfrac12,\tfrac12)$ within blocks. Invertibility first. Since
$A_2^{-1}\mathbf{1} = \mathbf{1}/(2a)$, the $D$-side factor is
$I - \gamma B^\top A_2^{-1} = I - \gamma\tfrac{z}{2a}
\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^\top$, whose eigenvalues are $1$ (on the difference
vector) and $1 - \gamma z/a$ (on $\mathbf{1}$); it is singular precisely
when $\gamma z = a$. The $A$-side factor
$I - \gamma\tfrac{z}{2d}\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^\top$ has eigenvalue
$1 - \gamma z/d > 0$, because $\gamma z \le 2c < d$; here $2c < d$ follows
from $4c^2 < ad \le d^2$. The common numerator in \eqref{eq:nuscalar} also
never vanishes: $ad - \gamma z^2 \ge ad - 4c^2 > 0$. For
\eqref{eq:nuscalar}: since $D_2$ is
exchangeable with row sums $2d$, $D_2^{-1}\mathbf{1} = \mathbf{1}/(2d)$, so
$\widehat B D_2^{-1} = \tfrac{z}{2d}\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^\top$ and
$\widehat B D_2^{-1} \widehat B^\top = \tfrac{z^2}{d}
\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^\top$ (using $\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^\top
\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^\top = 2\,\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^\top$). The matrix
$r = I - \gamma\tfrac{z}{2d}\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^\top$ satisfies
$\mathbf{1}^\top r^{-1} = \big(1 - \gamma z/d\big)^{-1}\mathbf{1}^\top$
(Sherman--Morrison), and $\operatorname{sym}$ does not change
$\mathbf{1}^\top(\cdot)\mathbf{1}$. With within weights
$(\tfrac12,\tfrac12)$, the fitness is
$\nu_{A_\gamma} = \tfrac14 \mathbf{1}^\top A_\gamma \mathbf{1}
= \tfrac14\,\dfrac{\mathbf{1}^\top A_2 \mathbf{1} - \gamma\tfrac{z^2}{d}
\cdot 4}{1 - \gamma z/d}
= \dfrac{a - \gamma z^2/d}{1 - \gamma z/d}
= \dfrac{ad - \gamma z^2}{d - \gamma z}$,
using $\mathbf{1}^\top A_2 \mathbf{1} = 4a$; the $D$-side is symmetric. For
\eqref{eq:xgz}: the inter-group ratio is $1/\nu_{A_\gamma} :
1/\nu_{D_\gamma}$, the common factor $ad - \gamma z^2$ cancels, and
$x_\gamma(z) = (d - \gamma z)/\big((d-\gamma z) + (a - \gamma z)\big)$. The
final sentence is the definition of $Q$ at the group level. Each identity
is also verified exactly by the certificate script at a grid of rational
$(\gamma, z)$ away from the singular point; at the singular point the
script verifies that the recursion is undefined (a division by an exact
zero) and that the recursion's weight approaches \eqref{eq:xgz} on both
sides.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Exact objective]
\label{prop:exactobj}
Let $x_* = x_1(c) = (d-c)/K$ be the population minimum-variance group
weight. Then $Q(x) = Q(x_*) + K\,(x - x_*)^2$ (exact square completion),
\begin{equation}
  x_\gamma(z) - x_* \;=\; \frac{(d-a)\,(\gamma z - c)}{(S - 2\gamma z)\,K},
  \label{eq:displacement}
\end{equation}
and therefore, with $r(u) = \big((u - c)/(S - 2u)\big)^2$,
\begin{equation}
  F_{\mathrm{ext}}(\gamma; e)
  \;=\; Q(x_*) \;+\; \frac{(d-a)^2}{K}\cdot
        \frac12 \sum_{\sigma = \pm 1} r\big(\gamma (c + \sigma e)\big).
  \label{eq:exactF}
\end{equation}
This is the exact expected out-of-sample variance of the extended
allocation; no Taylor surrogate is involved. It agrees with the objective
of the raw recursion at every $(\gamma, e)$ where the recursion is
defined, which by Lemma~\ref{lem:reduce} is everywhere except the single
point $\gamma = a/(c+e)$ when $e \ge a - c$. In
particular
\begin{equation}
  V_0(\gamma) = \frac{9\,(44\gamma^2 - 200\gamma + 275)}
  {5\,(8\gamma - 25)^2},
  \qquad
  G(\gamma) = \frac{1500\,\gamma^2\,(16\gamma + 1)}{(8\gamma - 25)^4},
  \label{eq:closedforms}
\end{equation}
where $G$ is the coefficient of $e^2$ in the small-noise expansion of
$F_{\mathrm{ext}}(\gamma; e)$; for $e < a - c$ there is no singular point
and $F_{\mathrm{ext}}$ is the raw objective outright.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
$Q$ is a quadratic in $x$ with leading coefficient $a + d - 2c = K$ and
vertex at $x_*$ (set $Q'(x) = 2ax - 2d(1-x) + 2c(1-2x) = 2K x - 2(d-c)$),
giving the square completion. For \eqref{eq:displacement}, put both terms of
$x_\gamma(z) - x_*$ over the common denominator $(S - 2\gamma z)K$:
the numerator is $(d - \gamma z)K - (d - c)(S - 2\gamma z)
= (\gamma z - c)(2d - S) = (d - a)(\gamma z - c)$, expanding and using
$K = S - 2c$. Then
$Q(x_\gamma(z)) - Q(x_*) = K (x_\gamma(z) - x_*)^2 =
\tfrac{(d-a)^2}{K}\, r(\gamma z)$, and averaging over
$z = c \pm e$ gives \eqref{eq:exactF}. The closed forms
\eqref{eq:closedforms} follow by substituting the family's numbers into
\eqref{eq:exactF}; they are verified exactly, at a grid of rational
$\gamma$, by the certificate script.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Exact interior optimum, monotone in the noise]
\label{thm:family}
Assume $a < d$, $c > 0$, $4c^2 < ad$, and $S > 6c$ (all satisfied by
\eqref{eq:familycov}: $4c^2 = \tfrac{36}{25} < \tfrac94 = ad$ and
$S = \tfrac{15}{4} > \tfrac{18}{5} = 6c$). Then for every noise amplitude
$0 < e \le c$, with $F_{\mathrm{ext}}$ the extended objective of
Lemma~\ref{lem:reduce}:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[\textup{(i)}] $F_{\mathrm{ext}}(\cdot\,; e)$ is strictly convex on
  $[0, 1]$;
\item[\textup{(ii)}] it has a unique minimizer $\gamma^*(e) \in
  (\tfrac12, 1)$ for $0 < e < c$, with the boundary values
  $\gamma^*(0) = 1$ and $\gamma^*(c) = \tfrac12$;
\item[\textup{(iii)}] $\gamma^*$ is strictly decreasing in $e$;
\item[\textup{(iv)}] the minimizer solves the exact first-order condition
\begin{equation}
  \sum_{\sigma = \pm 1}
  \frac{(c + \sigma e)\,\big[\gamma(c + \sigma e) - c\big]}
       {\big[S - 2\gamma(c + \sigma e)\big]^{3}} \;=\; 0,
  \label{eq:foc}
\end{equation}
and for small $e$,
\begin{equation}
  \gamma^*(e) \;=\; 1 - \frac{S + 4c}{c^2\,(S - 2c)}\; e^2 + O(e^4)
  \;=\; 1 - \frac{1025}{153}\, e^2 + O(e^4)
  \label{eq:smalle}
\end{equation}
for the numbers of \eqref{eq:familycov};
\item[\textup{(v)}] for the numbers of \eqref{eq:familycov} the minimizer
  lies strictly inside the regular region: whenever the singular point
  $\gamma_e = a/(c+e)$ of Lemma~\ref{lem:reduce} lies in $[0,1]$, that is
  for $e \ge a - c = \tfrac{3}{20}$,
\[
  \gamma^*(e) \;<\; \frac{a}{c + e}.
\]
Consequently the raw recursion is defined on a neighborhood of
$\gamma^*(e)$ and coincides there with $F_{\mathrm{ext}}$, so
(i)--(iv) describe the optimizer of the implemented recursion and not only
of the extension.
\end{enumerate}
Consequently every coupling in $[\tfrac12, 1]$ is optimal at exactly one
noise amplitude $e \in [0, c]$. In the noise parametrization of the paper,
$e = 2\tau$, so the theorem covers $0 < \tau \le \tfrac{3}{10}$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Throughout, drop the positive constant $(d-a)^2/K$ and study
$\bar F(\gamma; e) = \tfrac12[r(\gamma(c+e)) + r(\gamma(c-e))]$ with
$r(u) = (u-c)^2/(S-2u)^2$, defined for $u < S/2$; note
$\gamma z \le 2c < S/2$ on the admissible range, since $z \le c + e \le 2c$
and $S > 6c > 4c$. By \eqref{eq:exactF}, $\bar F$ is $F_{\mathrm{ext}}$ up
to a positive affine map, and it is smooth on all of $[0,1] \times [0,c]$;
the point $\gamma z = a$, where the raw recursion is undefined, is
unremarkable for $\bar F$.

\emph{Derivatives of $r$.} Direct differentiation gives
\[
  r'(u) \;=\; \frac{2K\,(u - c)}{(S - 2u)^3},
  \qquad
  r''(u) \;=\; \frac{2K\,\big(S - 6c + 4u\big)}{(S - 2u)^4}.
\]
(For $r'$: the quotient rule gives numerator
$2(u-c)(S-2u) + 4(u-c)^2 = 2(u-c)(S-2c)$; for $r''$, differentiate $r'$ and
simplify the numerator to $2K[(S-2u) + 6(u-c)]$.)

\emph{(i) Convexity.} $\partial^2_{\gamma\gamma}\bar F(\gamma;e) =
\tfrac12\sum_\sigma z_\sigma^2\, r''(\gamma z_\sigma)$ with
$z_\sigma = c + \sigma e \ge 0$. Since $S > 6c$ and $u = \gamma z_\sigma \ge
0$, the numerator $S - 6c + 4u$ is strictly positive, and $(S-2u)^4 > 0$, so
$r'' > 0$ wherever $z_\sigma > 0$; at $e = c$ the lower branch has
$z_- = 0$ and contributes nothing, while the upper branch still gives strict
positivity. Hence $\bar F$ is strictly convex in $\gamma$ on $[0,1]$.

\emph{Endpoint signs.} At $\gamma = 0$:
$\partial_\gamma \bar F(0; e) = \tfrac12 \sum_\sigma z_\sigma r'(0)
= r'(0)\, c = -\,2Kc^2/S^3 < 0$, independently of $e$. At $\gamma = 1$:
define $\psi(z) = z\, r'(z) = 2K z (z - c)/(S - 2z)^3$, so
$\partial_\gamma \bar F(1; e) = \tfrac12[\psi(c+e) + \psi(c-e)]$. One
computes
\[
  \psi''(z) \;=\; \frac{4K\,\big[\,S(S - 6c) + 4z\,(2S - 3c) + 4z^2\,\big]}
  {(S - 2z)^5} \;>\; 0
  \qquad (0 \le z \le 2c),
\]
since every bracketed term is positive under $S > 6c$ and $S - 2z \ge
S - 4c > 0$. Thus $\psi$ is strictly convex on $[0, 2c]$, and since
$\psi(c) = 0$, strict midpoint convexity gives
$\tfrac12[\psi(c+e) + \psi(c-e)] > \psi(c) = 0$ for $0 < e \le c$. So the
$\gamma$-derivative is strictly negative at $\gamma = 0$ and strictly
positive at $\gamma = 1$.

\emph{(ii) Unique interior minimizer.} Strict convexity plus the endpoint
signs give a unique root $\gamma^*(e) \in (0,1)$ of
$\partial_\gamma \bar F = 0$, which is the unique minimizer, and
\eqref{eq:foc} is $\partial_\gamma \bar F = 0$ written out. At $e = c$ the
lower branch has $z_- = 0$, so \eqref{eq:foc} reduces to
$r'(2c\gamma) = 0$, whose unique solution is $2c\gamma = c$, i.e.\
$\gamma^*(c) = \tfrac12$; and $\gamma^*(e) > \tfrac12$ for $e < c$ follows
from (iii). At $e = 0$ both branches coincide, $\partial_\gamma \bar F
\propto r'(\gamma c)\,c$, whose root is $\gamma = 1$; this is the
noise-free optimum, consistent with $V_0'(1) = 0$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:mv}
applies: the reduction reaches the group-level minimum variance at
$\gamma = 1$ exactly).

\emph{(iii) Monotonicity.} For fixed $\gamma > 0$ write
$\psi_\gamma(z) = z\, r'(\gamma z)$, so that
$\partial_\gamma \bar F(\gamma; e) =
\tfrac12\big[\psi_\gamma(c+e) + \psi_\gamma(c-e)\big]$ and
\[
  \partial_e \partial_\gamma \bar F(\gamma; e) \;=\;
  \tfrac{1}{2}\big[\psi_\gamma'(c+e) - \psi_\gamma'(c-e)\big].
\]
The identity $\psi_\gamma(z) = \psi(\gamma z)/\gamma$ gives
$\psi_\gamma''(z) = \gamma\, \psi''(\gamma z) > 0$ on the admissible range,
so $\psi_\gamma'$ is strictly increasing,
$\psi_\gamma'(c+e) > \psi_\gamma'(c-e)$, and the mixed partial is strictly
positive. By the implicit function theorem on \eqref{eq:foc},
$d\gamma^*/de = -\,\partial_e\partial_\gamma \bar F \,/\,
\partial^2_{\gamma\gamma}\bar F < 0$.

\emph{(iv) Expansion.} Insert $\gamma = 1 - \delta$ into \eqref{eq:foc} and
expand to first order in $\delta$ and $e^2$. The $e$-expansion of the sum at
$\gamma = 1$ is
$\tfrac{2 e^2 (S + 4c)}{K^4} + O(e^4)$ (expand each branch of
\eqref{eq:foc} in $e$; odd terms cancel), while the $\delta$-derivative at
$e = 0$ is $-\,2c^2/K^3 + O(\delta, e^2)$. Setting the sum of the two
contributions to zero gives $\delta = e^2 (S + 4c)/(c^2 K) + O(e^4)$, which
is \eqref{eq:smalle}; the coefficient evaluates to
$\tfrac{1025}{153}$ for \eqref{eq:familycov}.

\emph{(v) The singular point lies uphill.} Let
$\gamma_e = a/(c+e) \le 1$ and write $z_\pm = c \pm e$, so that
$\gamma_e z_+ = a$ and $\gamma_e z_- = a z_-/z_+$. By strict convexity it
suffices to show $\partial_\gamma \bar F(\gamma_e; e) > 0$. Substituting
into $\partial_\gamma \bar F = \tfrac12\sum_\sigma z_\sigma\,
r'(\gamma z_\sigma)$ and clearing the inner fractions,
\[
  \partial_\gamma \bar F(\gamma_e; e)
  \;=\; K\left[\frac{z_+\,(a-c)}{(S-2a)^3}
  \;+\; \frac{z_-\,(a z_- - c z_+)\, z_+^2}{(S z_+ - 2a z_-)^3}\right].
\]
Both denominators are positive: $S - 2a = \tfrac94$ for
\eqref{eq:familycov}, and
$S z_+ - 2a z_- \ge z_+ (S - 2a) > 0$ since $z_- \le z_+$. Multiplying by
the positive quantity $(S-2a)^3 (S z_+ - 2a z_-)^3 / K$ preserves the sign
and produces the polynomial
\[
  P(e) \;=\; z_+ (a-c)\,(S z_+ - 2a z_-)^3
  \;+\; z_- (a z_- - c z_+)\, z_+^2\, (S - 2a)^3,
\]
which for the numbers of \eqref{eq:familycov} expands to
\[
  P(e) \;=\; \tfrac{177147}{400000} \;+\; \tfrac{6561}{800}\, e^2
  \;+\; \tfrac{1215}{32}\, e^3 \;+\; \tfrac{23733}{640}\, e^4 .
\]
Every coefficient is nonnegative and the constant term is positive, so
$P(e) > 0$ for every $e \ge 0$, and in particular on
$[\tfrac{3}{20}, \tfrac35]$. Hence
$\partial_\gamma \bar F(\gamma_e; e) > 0$, and by strict convexity
$\gamma^*(e) < \gamma_e$. The expansion of $P$ and the sign agreement with
the first-order condition are verified in exact rational arithmetic by the
certificate script.

The final sentence of the theorem follows
from (ii)--(iii): $\gamma^* : [0, c] \to [\tfrac12, 1]$ is a continuous
strictly decreasing bijection.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[The surrogate frontier spans the whole bridge in this
family]
\label{cor:famsurr}
For the family \eqref{eq:familycov}, the closed forms
\eqref{eq:closedforms} satisfy $V_0' < 0$, $V_0'' > 0$, $G' > 0$, $G'' > 0$
on $(0,1)$, and
\[
  t(\gamma) \;=\; -\frac{V_0'(\gamma)}{G'(\gamma)}
  \;=\; \frac{9\,(1 - \gamma)\,(25 - 8\gamma)^2}
        {25\,\gamma\,(64\gamma^2 + 608\gamma + 25)}
\]
decreases strictly from $+\infty$ (at $\gamma \downarrow 0$) to $0$ (at
$\gamma \uparrow 1$). In the language of Theorem~\ref{thm:frontier} below,
$t_0 = \infty$ and $t_1 = 0$: for this family, and only because of these
endpoint limits, the surrogate optimizer sweeps the entire open bridge and
neither endpoint is surrogate-optimal at any positive finite noise level.
The surrogate optimizer is not the exact optimizer at finite noise. At
$e = c$ the exact minimizer is $\tfrac12$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:family}), while
$t(\tfrac12) = \tfrac{1323}{2875} \ne e^2 = \tfrac{9}{25}$ and the
surrogate optimizer sits near $0.537$. The two agree to order $e^2$ as
$e \to 0$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:transfer}); what they share exactly is the
sweep of the whole open bridge.
\end{corollary}

\begin{remark}
Theorem~\ref{thm:family} reproduces, exactly, the numerical optimizer sweep
previously obtained by brute force on this family: with $e = 2\tau$, the
exact minimizers at $\tau = 0.10, 0.15, 0.20, 0.25, 0.30$ are
$\gamma^* \approx 0.8308,\ 0.7237,\ 0.6339,\ 0.5603,\ 0.5000$, the last
being exact. The value $\tau = 0.35$ falls outside the range $e \le c$
covered by the theorem (one noise branch then has negative estimated
cross covariance); it could be handled by direct polynomial certification
but we do not pursue it.
\end{remark}

\section{The surrogate in general}
\label{sec:frontier}

Away from solvable families one can still organize the problem through the
second-order surrogate $F_2 = V_0 + \tau^2 G$ of \eqref{eq:expansion}.

\begin{theorem}[Clipped monotone frontier, for the surrogate]
\label{thm:frontier}
Suppose $V_0$ and $G$ are continuous on $[0, \bar\gamma]$ and $C^2$ on
$(0, \bar\gamma)$, with: $V_0' < 0$; $G' > 0$; $V_0'' \ge 0$ and
$G'' \ge 0$; and at every $\gamma$ at least one of $V_0''(\gamma) > 0$,
$G''(\gamma) > 0$ holds. Define $t = -V_0'/G'$ on $(0,\bar\gamma)$ and its
endpoint limits $t_0 = \lim_{\gamma \downarrow 0} t(\gamma) \in (0, \infty]$
and $t_1 = \lim_{\gamma \uparrow \bar\gamma} t(\gamma) \in [0, \infty)$.
Then $t$ is continuous and strictly decreasing, and for every
$s = \tau^2 > 0$ the surrogate $F_2(\cdot\,; \tau)$ has the unique minimizer
\begin{equation}
  \gamma_2^*(s) \;=\;
  \begin{cases}
    \bar\gamma, & s \le t_1, \\
    t^{-1}(s), & t_1 < s < t_0, \\
    0, & s \ge t_0,
  \end{cases}
  \label{eq:clipped}
\end{equation}
which is nonincreasing in $s$. In particular the endpoints are optimal for
the surrogate on nontrivial intervals of noise levels precisely when
$t_1 > 0$, respectively $t_0 < \infty$. If moreover
$V_0, G \in C^1([0,\bar\gamma])$ with $V_0'(0) < 0$, as holds under
Assumption~\ref{ass:reg} via \eqref{eq:expansion}, then
$t_0 = -V_0'(0)/G'(0)$, finite exactly when $G'(0) > 0$. Endpoint
regularity is needed for that formula: $V_0(\gamma) = V_0(0) - \sqrt\gamma$
near $0$, with smooth $G$, satisfies every open-interval hypothesis and
forces $t_0 = \infty$ regardless of $G'(0)$. In
Example~\ref{ex:boundary}, $t_0 = \tfrac{27}{800}$; in the family of
Section~\ref{sec:family}, $t_0 = \infty$ and $t_1 = 0$
(Corollary~\ref{cor:famsurr}).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Strict monotonicity of $t$: on $(0,\bar\gamma)$,
\[
  t' \;=\; \frac{-V_0''\, G' + V_0'\, G''}{(G')^2},
\]
where the first numerator term is $\le 0$ (since $V_0'' \ge 0$, $G' > 0$)
and the second is $\le 0$ (since $V_0' < 0$, $G'' \ge 0$); at each $\gamma$
at least one is strictly negative by hypothesis, so $t' < 0$ pointwise, and
$t$ is a continuous strictly decreasing bijection from $(0,\bar\gamma)$ onto
$(t_1, t_0)$. Sign analysis of $F_2' = V_0' + s\, G'$: since $G' > 0$,
$F_2'(\gamma) < 0 \iff s < t(\gamma)$. If $t_1 < s < t_0$ then
$F_2' < 0$ on $(0, t^{-1}(s))$ and $F_2' > 0$ on $(t^{-1}(s), \bar\gamma)$,
so $t^{-1}(s)$ is the unique minimizer. If $s \le t_1$ then $s < t(\gamma)$
for every interior $\gamma$, so $F_2' < 0$ throughout and the minimum sits
at $\bar\gamma$; if $s \ge t_0$ then $F_2' > 0$ throughout and the minimum
sits at $0$. Monotonicity of \eqref{eq:clipped} in $s$ follows from the
monotonicity of $t^{-1}$ and the ordering of the three regimes.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Local transfer to the exact objective]
\label{prop:transfer}
Fix $\tau$ and let $I \subseteq [0, \bar\gamma]$ be an interval containing
$\gamma_2^*(\tau^2)$ on which $F_2'' \ge c > 0$ and
$|F - F_2| \le K\tau^4$. If $\gamma_F$ minimizes $F$ over $I$, then
\[
  |\gamma_F - \gamma_2^*| \;\le\; 2\sqrt{K/c}\;\tau^2 .
\]
No claim is made beyond $I$: uniqueness, global optimality, and the
monotonicity of Theorem~\ref{thm:frontier} are statements about the
surrogate.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Strong convexity of $F_2$ on the interval $I$ gives, for the minimizer
$\gamma_2^*$ of $F_2$,
$\tfrac{c}{2}(\gamma_F - \gamma_2^*)^2 \le F_2(\gamma_F) -
F_2(\gamma_2^*)$. Replace $F_2$ by $F$ twice, paying $K\tau^4$ each time:
$F_2(\gamma_F) - F_2(\gamma_2^*) \le F(\gamma_F) - F(\gamma_2^*) +
2K\tau^4$. Finally $F(\gamma_F) - F(\gamma_2^*) \le 0$ because $\gamma_F$
minimizes $F$ over $I$ and $\gamma_2^* \in I$. Combine and solve for
$|\gamma_F - \gamma_2^*|$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The same dial in meteorology]
\label{rem:weather}
The trade-off studied here is not particular to finance. The damped Schur
complement \eqref{eq:family} coincides, term for term, with the reliability
shrinkage that keeps Gaussian (Vecchia) pseudo-likelihoods of
high-dimensional weather fields estimable when stations outnumber
observations \cite{cotton2026damping}. In that language $V_0$ is the loss
of a spatial model that trusts its estimated conditional structure, $\tau$
is the sampling depth, and the clipped frontier of
Theorem~\ref{thm:frontier} says how much conditional structure to trust at
a given depth. The spatial literature also fits the damping intensity from
data, which supplies a practical estimate of the noise level $s = \tau^2$
at which to read \eqref{eq:clipped}; the correspondence and a comparison of
recipes are developed in \cite{cotton2026damping}.
\end{remark}

\section{A one-entry example}
\label{sec:witness}

The solved family relies on the exchangeability of common cross-block
noise. The next example removes that symmetry: the noise sits in a single
covariance entry, and the endpoint comparisons are certified exactly.

\begin{example}[An interior minimizer, exactly, without common-noise
symmetry]
\label{ex:interior}
Take the covariance \eqref{eq:familycov} and let the estimate err in the
$(1,3)$ entry only, by $\pm \tfrac{3}{20}\cdot 2$ with probability
$\tfrac12$ each. Then, in exact rational arithmetic,
\[
  F(\tfrac{9}{10}) < F(0) \ \ \text{(margin } 4.879\times10^{-2}\text{)},
  \qquad
  F(\tfrac{9}{10}) < F(1) \ \ \text{(margin } 5.702\times10^{-4}\text{)},
\]
so every minimizer of $F$ on $[0,1]$ is strictly interior. Because both
inequalities are strict and $F$ depends continuously on the covariance and
noise parameters at each fixed $\gamma$, the same conclusion holds on an
open neighborhood of these parameters: the phenomenon is robust, not a
knife-edge rational coincidence. This is also the minimal non-trivial
configuration: because nodes with a singleton child perform no
augmentation, $n \le 3$ admits no $\gamma$-dependence at all, and $n = 4$ in
two blocks of two is the smallest universe on which the bridge exists.
\end{example}
\begin{proof}
$F(0)$, $F(\tfrac{9}{10})$, $F(1)$ are exact rational numbers, each a finite
composition of field operations \eqref{eq:aug} on rational inputs, and the
two inequalities are verified in exact fraction arithmetic by the
certificate script. Three values settle the whole interval as follows.
Every denominator arising in the recursion is a polynomial in $\gamma$ with
rational coefficients; the script certifies each strictly positive on
$[0,1]$, on both noise branches, by exhibiting positive Bernstein
coefficients (with exact subdivision where needed). Hence $F$ is continuous
on $[0,1]$ and attains its minimum, and
$\min F \le F(\tfrac{9}{10}) < \min\{F(0), F(1)\}$, so no minimizer is an
endpoint. The same positivity gives the positive definiteness of the
augmented blocks for every $\gamma$. Robustness: at each of the three
$\gamma$ values, $F$ is a continuous (rational) function of the covariance
entries and the noise amplitude on a neighborhood where the certified
denominators remain positive, so both strict inequalities persist under
sufficiently small parameter perturbations.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[A structural cancellation, special to these families]
\label{rem:cancel}
In both noise models on the covariance \eqref{eq:familycov} --- the common
cross-correlation noise of Section~\ref{sec:family} and the one-entry noise
of Example~\ref{ex:interior} --- the boundary derivative does not move with
the noise: exact jet evaluation gives
\[
  \partial_\gamma F(0;\tau) \;=\; V_0'(0) \;=\; -\tfrac{216}{3125}
  \qquad\text{exactly, at } \tau \in \{\tfrac{1}{10}, \tfrac{3}{20},
  \tfrac15\},
\]
in both models. In the notation of \eqref{eq:expansion}, $G'(0) = 0$ for
these families; for the solved family this is also visible in
\eqref{eq:closedforms}, where $G(\gamma) \propto \gamma^2(16\gamma+1)$
vanishes quadratically. That is a cancellation particular to these
families, not a general fact: Example~\ref{ex:boundary} has
$G'(0) = \tfrac{8}{189} > 0$. It explains why numerical experiments on
these families alone can suggest, wrongly, that the noise cost is
$O(\gamma^2\tau^2)$ in general.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Sign, boundedness, and shorting]
\label{rem:bounded}
The sign of $F - V_0$ is not fixed: symmetric noise can reduce expected
out-of-sample variance slightly at moderate $\gamma$. Nor does the noise
cost blow up while Assumption~\ref{ass:reg} holds: the weights are a
continuous function on the compact set $K$, hence bounded, say
$\|w\| \le L$, and $w^\top \Sigma w \le \lambda_{\max}(\Sigma) L^2$. A
tempting sharper bound fails. The weights sum to one but are not always
long-only, so $w^\top \Sigma w \le \lambda_{\max}(\Sigma)$ is not
available. The solved family makes this exact: at $e = c$ on the upper
noise branch, $\gamma = 1$ gives the group weight $x_1(2c) = \tfrac43$ and
asset weights $(\tfrac23, \tfrac23, -\tfrac16, -\tfrac16)$. The estimated
covariance there is still positive definite; shorting the volatile block is
simply what minimum variance does once the estimated cross covariance
exceeds the smaller block variance. What breaks at large noise is also
worth stating precisely. The allocation is a rational function of its
inputs and becomes undefined only where a denominator vanishes, as at the
point $\gamma z = a$ of Lemma~\ref{lem:reduce}. Leaving the
positive-definite cone does not by itself undefine the recursion; it
removes the variance interpretation of the fitness, and implementations
must repair or refuse.
\end{remark}

\section{Closing}

Under cross-block estimation error the Schur coupling is a bias--variance
dial, and the three layers say when to turn it. Small noise moves the
optimum uniformly away from hierarchical risk parity whenever coupling has
population value (Theorem~\ref{thm:nohrp}), at a cost of order
$\gamma\tau^2$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:noisecost}). At an exact minimum-variance
endpoint, positive marginal noise sensitivity pushes the optimum inside by
order $\tau^2$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:mvlocal}). And in the solved family the
exact optimizer is unique, interior, and slides monotonically from full
coupling at zero noise to the middle of the bridge at the largest
admissible noise (Theorem~\ref{thm:family}). The exact examples mark what
cannot be claimed in general: hierarchical risk parity can become locally
optimal at a finite noise level (Example~\ref{ex:boundary}), and the
surrogate frontier is a clipped inverse, with each endpoint owning an
interval of noise levels unless its limit condition holds
(Theorem~\ref{thm:frontier}). A walk-forward bridge on Dow constituents is
interactive at \texttt{schur.microprediction.org}; its minimum sits near
$\gamma \approx 0.55$ and survives Ledoit--Wolf shrinkage of the inputs.
Every theorem in this paper has a live check at
\texttt{schur.microprediction.org/theorems.html}, computed from the same
recursion.
Neither school dominates the other. Each may own an interval of noise
levels, and between them the border is drawn at $\tau^2 = -V_0'/G'$.
