Example 1 — HRP becomes locally optimal at finite noise

For Family B (blocks A = D = [[1,−½], [−½,2]], cross entries 0.1, noise ±τ in one cross entry) the paper computes the boundary derivative exactly: ∂γF(0;τ) = −1/700 + (8/189)τ². The dots are measured from the recursion; the line is the formula. The sign flips at τ* = √(27/800) ≈ 0.1837: beyond it, moving off HRP hurts, so the HRP endpoint is a one-sided local minimum despite coupling having population value.

What to look for: measured dots sit exactly on the parabola; the zero crossing is at τ* ≈ 0.1837.
