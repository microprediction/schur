Effective damping — and what happens near rank deficiency

A common γ does not damp every cluster equally. Each knot's exposure interpolates between its NCO value and its minimum-variance value, and the interpolation actually achieved is λ = γr / (1 − γ + γr), where r is the share of the knot's variance that the other knots leave unexplained. A knot the others nearly explain has small r and barely moves until γ is very close to 1.

λ(γ) for four values of r. At r = 1 the knot is unpredictable from the others and λ = γ. As r falls the curve hugs zero and then rises in a boundary layer at the right end.
What to look for: every curve passes through λ = ½ at γ = 1/(1 + r), so the r = 0.01 curve reaches the halfway point only at γ ≈ 0.990.

The limit r → 0 is a duplicated knot. Take two clusters, each a knot and an independent asset, all of unit variance, with the two knots correlated at 1/(1 + ε). The optimum has variance 1/3 as ε → 0. The bridge reaches it at γ = 1 for every ε > 0, but at every fixed γ < 1 its variance tends to 3/8. The limits do not commute.

What to look for: as ε shrinks, the γ-curve stays flat at 3/8 over more and more of the bridge and drops to 1/3 in a narrower layer at the right end. The dashed path in effective exposure, κ̅(λ) = (1 − λ)/s + λh with h = S+1, is the same at every ε: it runs continuously from 3/8 to 1/3, which is the paper's Proposition on a bridge in effective exposure.

Both curves are computed from the compressed two-tier formulas of the paper's Section 6, with the knot covariance 11′ + εI and unit member precision. The verification script checks the same limits.
