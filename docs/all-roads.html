All roads lead to minimum variance

Five heuristics, one dial each, one floor.

Inverse variance, HRP, hierarchical minimum variance, HERC and NCO look like different families. Each is the near end of a Schur bridge: turn one dial and every one of them lands on the same optimizer. With an estimated covariance the best place to stand is often part way across.

drag to orbit · scroll to zoom

    What the axes are

    Twenty-four assets in four correlated sectors, one factor per sector. Height is how much worse a portfolio is than the far-end optimum, in percent of its objective: variance for minimum variance, the inverse squared diversification ratio for maximum diversification, the inverse squared Sharpe ratio for tangency. The violet floor is the optimum itself, at zero.

    Depth is the method. Along the length of each ribbon the dial runs from 0, the method as published, to 1, the far end. The dial damps a Schur complement: it conditions each cluster on the assets outside it, and, for HERC, each asset on its cluster mates.

    How to read it

    With the true covariance every ribbon descends to the floor. That is the theorem: at dial 1 every one of these recipes is the global optimizer, whatever its partition. The ribbons differ only in where they start.

    Switch to an estimated covariance and the recipes see noise. The far ends lift off the floor, because the estimated optimizer is not the true one, and some ribbons now dip before they end. The lowest point is where to stand, and it is neither end.

    The HERC square view shows HERC with both of its dials, the cluster dial across and the asset dial along. HERC is the near corner; minimum variance is the far corner; the surface between is the square.
