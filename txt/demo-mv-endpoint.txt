 Theorem 3 — small noise pushes the optimum off the minimum-variance end

 At an exact minimum-variance endpoint the population curve is flat
 (V&#8320;&prime;(1) = 0), so any positive marginal noise sensitivity wins
 locally and the optimizer moves inside by order &tau;&sup2;:
 1 &minus; &gamma;*(&tau;) &asymp; (G&prime;(1)/V&#8320;&Prime;(1))&tau;&sup2;.
 For Family A the paper computes the coefficient exactly: with e = 2&tau;,
 1 &minus; &gamma;* &asymp; (1025/153)e&sup2;.

 Dots: minimizers of the exact F found by search, plotted
 as 1 &minus; &gamma;* against e&sup2;. Line: slope 1025/153 from the
 theorem.

 What to look for: the dots approach the line as
 e &darr; 0. The theorem is a small-noise statement, and the curvature away from
 the line at larger e is the O(e&#8308;) term.
